URA4 LOSS FREQUENCY CALCULATOR

Analyse ura4-loss fluctuation assays using independent cultures and paired YES and YES + 5-FOA plating data. The primary analysis follows the Lea–Coulson median approach used by Zaratiegui et al. (2011). When only a fraction of each culture is plated on 5-FOA, the calculator uses a partial-plating-aware Luria–Delbrück likelihood model instead of applying the uncorrected Lea–Coulson equation to the plated counts. The calculator also reports per-culture resistant-cell frequencies and optional eviction/conversion characterization.

CALCULATION LOGIC

Per-culture resistant-cell frequency: (5-FOA colonies / cells represented on 5-FOA) ÷ (YES colonies / cells represented on YES). This descriptive frequency is corrected for the two plating targets and is calculated separately for each independent culture.

Fluctuation analysis: the Lea–Coulson median estimator uses the distribution of 5-FOA-resistant colony counts across independent cultures to estimate the mean number of mutation events per culture (m). The mutation rate is estimated as μ = m / Nt, where Nt is the final viable population size. The YES control estimates the viable fraction: Nt = measured total cell concentration × culture volume × (YES colonies / YES target cells).

Partial plating: the plated fraction is calculated as 5-FOA target cells ÷ total cells in the culture. The final viable population used to calculate μ is corrected separately using the YES control. When only a fraction is plated, the calculator uses the Luria–Delbrück distribution with binomial thinning to account for each culture’s actual plated fraction. This is a partial-plating-aware maximum-likelihood extension, not an uncorrected Lea–Coulson estimate.

Confidence intervals: the descriptive median frequency uses an exact binomial order-statistic interval. The fluctuation-model estimate uses a 95% profile-likelihood interval when partial plating is used. Zaratiegui et al. report 95% confidence intervals based on a binomial distribution.

The Eviction / Conversion Characterization is calculated separately and describes the composition of the ura4-loss events; it does not affect the ura4 loss frequency or the statistical comparisons.

Plating data can be transferred from the plating calculator. Use “Send to ura4-loss calculator” on that page, then import them here.

The table reports the median per-culture resistant-cell frequency and its exact 95% order-statistic confidence interval. Mean, SD and SEM are also shown for transparency; the mean is not used as the fluctuation-test estimator.

ZARATIEGUI-STYLE FLUCTUATION ANALYSIS

Lea–Coulson median approach

Zaratiegui et al. (2011) used 12 independent cultures per strain, calculated mutation frequencies with the Lea–Coulson median estimator, and reported 95% confidence intervals based on a binomial distribution. Their supplementary methods also describe rich-medium dilution plates to account for cell death and plating efficiency. The supplement does not specify the exact fraction of each culture plated on 5-FOA. The classical Lea–Coulson equation is used when the complete culture is represented on selective medium. For partial plating, this calculator instead fits the standard Luria–Delbrück distribution after binomial thinning by each culture’s plated fraction. This is an explicit partial-plating extension and accommodates different plated fractions and final viable population sizes.

Partial-plating model: the number of 5-FOA-resistant colonies observed on a plate is treated as a random sample of the mutant population in that culture. The likelihood accounts for the fraction of the culture represented on that plate; it does not simply multiply the observed colony count by the inverse plated fraction.

Optional analysis: mlemur (maximum-likelihood method)

mlemur: mlemur (MLE MUtation Rate calculator) uses maximum-likelihood methods to estimate mutation rates from the distribution of mutant counts across independent cultures. Unlike the classical Lea–Coulson method of the median, it can model several features that may affect fluctuation assays, including partial plating, cell death, phenotypic lag, variation in final population size and mutations arising after the exponential-growth phase. The model must be selected to match the experiment; these options should not be enabled automatically without supporting experimental information.

For this assay, each of the 12 cultures is an independent lineage started from a separate colony, grown overnight, refreshed separately and then plated. The 5-FOA colony counts, viable-cell measurements and actual plated fractions are therefore needed for each culture. A single-cell population estimate or a simple correction of colony counts by the plated fraction is not a substitute for the partial-plating likelihood model.

Important: This browser calculator does not reproduce the mlemur likelihood engine. It prepares the per-culture data for analysis in the published mlemur software and lets you record the resulting mutation-rate estimates and confidence intervals here. This avoids presenting an unvalidated in-browser approximation as an mlemur result.

STATISTICAL COMPARISONS

Define only the biological comparisons you want to test. The statistics apply to per-culture ura4-loss frequency only. The calculator uses a Mann–Whitney U test and applies a Bonferroni correction across the comparisons entered, matching the approach reported by Zaratiegui et al. Eviction/conversion data are not used for statistical testing.

EVICTION / CONVERSION CHARACTERIZATION

Optional. Enter the number of characterized colonies for each strain. Leave blank when these data are not available. Blank means missing data; it is not treated as zero.
